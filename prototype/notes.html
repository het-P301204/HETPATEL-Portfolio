Notes

Things I noticed and wanted to keep. Shorter than an investigation, and not the same shape — a note is one observation that did not need working out.

The notes

  1. 2026-07-22

    Two scanners disagree on the same TLS configuration. One reports the cipher order as offered, the other as negotiated. Neither is wrong — they are answering different questions, and the tool that lets you tell which one it answered is the more useful tool.

  2. 2026-06-30

    Evidence that satisfies one framework often fails another, because the two define the control boundary in different places. I had assumed the overlap between frameworks was mostly a mapping exercise. It is not; the same document can be sufficient under one and incomplete under the other, and nothing in either document tells you that.

  3. 2026-06-11

    Why do allow-lists so often normalise after matching rather than before? I have now seen it twice and I do not know whether it is a common default, a performance argument, or a coincidence. Worth finding out.

    2026-08-02 — followed up. Not resolved, but narrowed: on a clean install the ordering is the stock default, so nobody chose it in the case I looked at. Why an allow-list kept failing open.

  4. 2026-05-14

    Trying to correlate two log sources by timestamp, and why it did not work: both were writing local time with no zone, from hosts configured differently. The correlation was fine. The clocks were not comparable.

  5. 2026-04-03

    A remediation note that says “sanitise user input” has told the reader nothing they did not know and given them nowhere to start. The useful version names the parameter, the sink, and the one change that closes it.

    2026-05-19 — superseded. This is right about the note and wrong about the reason. The problem is not vagueness; it is that the note was addressed to somebody who already agreed with it.

  6. 2026-03-18

    Rate limiting behaved differently under parallel requests than under the same number of sequential ones, which means every enumeration result I had recorded that week was measuring the limiter rather than the host.
