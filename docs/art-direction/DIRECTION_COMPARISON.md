## A · SIGNAL — dark premium / technical instrument

**OBSERVED at 1:1, 1280.** Display type at 104px Geist, `−0.035em`, four masked lines rising on
entrance. Mono eyebrow carries role and employer above the headline. A hairline-separated mono
index on the right numbers the four things he actually does. Below: statement left, three-row
role block right. Work rows are full-bleed hover with a signal-orange number and arrow.

**What works.** The strongest *premium* hit of the four and the best five-second impression. The
headline is a point of view rather than a label — the brief's actual requirement. Dark ground plus
one hot accent reads expensive without a single cliché. The three-cell strip at the bottom
(Method / Assurance / Honestly) is the best use of the attribution ceiling in any direction: the
honesty becomes a *designed element* instead of a disclaimer.

**What does not.** The two dim headline lines sit at `#9B9A94` on `#0B0C0E` and **recede too far —
the bottom half of the hero reads as unfinished rather than as deliberate de-emphasis.** The
right-hand index is pushed hard to the viewport edge and detaches from the headline it belongs to.
The employer is in a 12px eyebrow — too quiet for the strongest credibility fact on the page.

**JUDGMENT: strongest overall. Fixable weaknesses, all compositional rather than systemic.**

---

## B · LEDGER — editorial-technical, light, left rail

**OBSERVED at fit, 1280.** Persistent left rail carrying a stacked serif name, positioning and
vertical nav. Wide content area. 100px Instrument Serif headline with an italic accent. Facts in a
mono/text two-column list. Work rows expand a framing sentence on hover.

**What works.** The prettiest single moment across all four — *"A finding and a control are not the
`same object`"* with the italic in oxide red is genuinely arresting. The hover-expanding framing
sentence is the best interaction idea produced: the index stays scannable and reveals reasoning on
demand. The persistent rail is a real answer to "identity across pages."

**What does not.** **It is the closest of the four to the thing being rejected.** Serif hero, warm
paper, prose-forward — it is a better-executed version of the same register, not a different one.
The rail is too narrow and its identity too small to function as a brand anchor; at 1280 the name
in it is 24px, smaller than any nav item in direction A. The oxide accent plus warm paper edges
toward literary magazine.

**JUDGMENT: the best craft, the wrong brief.** Harvest the hover-expand interaction and the rail
concept; do not carry the register.

---

## C · FIELD — refined monochrome, oversized wordmark

**OBSERVED at fit, 1280.** HET at 170px flush left, PATEL flush right in grey, `−0.055em`
uppercase Inter Tight. A hairline lane strip beneath. Blue-flood hover on work rows. A near-black
experience band with mono capability tags.

**What works.** **The most memorable image of the four by a wide margin.** If the memorability test
is "what would they remember," this is the only direction with an answer that is not "the writing."
The name genuinely becomes a visual element, which is exactly what the brief asked for. The
inverted experience band gives professional history real weight. `mix-blend-mode: difference` on
the fixed bar is a nice piece of craft — the nav inverts against whatever passes under it.

**What does not.** **The first viewport contains almost no cybersecurity signal.** A recruiter sees
an enormous name and a 12px mono line. That fails success criterion 3 and the 5-second test —
"this person has a point of view" becomes "this person has a big name." The blue (`#1B4DFF`) flood
on hover is closer to product-startup than security. Tallest of the four at 2,216px because the
wordmark eats a full screen.

**JUDGMENT: strongest identity, weakest signal.** The wordmark idea is the single most valuable
asset produced and it needs to be compressed and paired with immediate signal.

## D · CASEFILE — evidence-led, density-rich

**OBSERVED at fit, 1280.** Identity compressed into a header band: name at 52px, one positioning
sentence, nav right. Immediately below, a five-cell mono readout — Role / Since / Regulated /
Frameworks / In progress. Then the investigations at full weight, each with framing sentence,
status dot, span, and a revision note.

**What works.** **By far the strongest hiring composition.** Every credibility fact — Info eShield,
January 2026, CSCRF, IRDAI, ISO 27001, Security+ — is above 900px and scannable in one pass. It
nails the 20-second and 2-minute reactions outright. `Conclusion revised twice` on the first case
is visible on the homepage, which surfaces the site's single most distinctive asset earlier than
any other direction. Shortest page at 1,490px. Best information typography.

**What does not.** **The hero is not a hero.** 52px is a heading; there is no moment of presence
and nothing memorable. **And the readout strip is the one element in the whole exercise that flirts
with a banned pattern** — a bordered grid of labelled metric cells is a dashboard shape. It survives
here only because every cell holds a plain fact rather than a number, and that is a thin margin.

**JUDGMENT: strongest content strategy, weakest art direction.**

## The finding the scores make obvious

**No single direction wins, and the two failure modes are exact opposites.**

- **A and C** win the first five seconds and under-serve the next twenty.
- **D** wins the next twenty and has no first five seconds.
- **B** is the best-crafted and is closest to the register already rejected.

The strongest artefacts produced are in different files:

| Asset | From | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Statement-led display hero with a point of view | **A** | The only hero that says something rather than labelling someone |
| Mono coordinate index | **A** | Technical signal with zero cliché |
| The honesty strip as designed elements | **A** | Turns the attribution ceiling into a feature |
| Oversized wordmark | **C** | The only genuinely memorable image |
| Compressed credential readout | **D** | Every hiring fact above the fold |
| Framing sentence + status + revision on the index row | **D** | Surfaces the revision register from the homepage |
| Hover-expand framing | **B** | Scannable index that reveals reasoning on demand |

## Recommendation for the next round

**Take A and D forward. They are the two highest scorers and, more importantly, they fail in
opposite directions — which means the hybrid is a real design, not a compromise.**

**A′ — SIGNAL, with CASEFILE's evidence density.** Keep A's dark ground, Geist display hero and
mono index. Fix the dim-line contrast. Pull D's credential readout up under the hero as a hairline
strip, not a bordered grid, so it clears the dashboard risk. Adopt D's index row — framing sentence,
status, revision note.

**D′ — CASEFILE, with a real hero.** Keep D's evidence-led structure and readout. Give the identity
band actual presence: the name at display scale using C's wordmark treatment, compressed to a band
rather than a screen. Replace the bordered grid with hairline rules.

**Direction C's wordmark should be tested inside both**, because it is the only answer to the
memorability test that does not depend on Het's writing.

**Direction B is not carried forward as a system.** Its hover-expand interaction is.

## Honest answer to the standing question

> "Would I confidently put this website in front of a Google / Microsoft / Apple / Cloudflare
> security hiring manager?"

**Not yet.** These are four homepages. None has an investigation page, an About page, a validated
mobile composition, a tested entrance sequence, or a genericity retest. Direction A at 1280 is the
first thing in this project that looks like it was art-directed, and that is a real change from the
rejected build — but a homepage is not a website.

**What is already true:** the density problem is solved, the typographic register is corrected, and
the development metadata is gone from the interface.

**What is not yet true:** nothing has been validated at 375 · 768 · 1024 · 1440, motion exists only
as entrance animation, and the strongest two directions have not been merged and re-rendered.
