# Brand Vocabulary

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 2) · **Status:** PROPOSED — Het selects the primary labels.

Three constraints govern every choice: **no false seniority**, **no corporate jargon**, **no hacker language**.

---

## 1. Core content labels

| Concept | **Recommended** | Alternatives | Rejected, and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary unit | **Investigation** | Case, Inquiry | *Project* (implies completion, generic default) · *Engagement* (consultancy-flavoured, excludes personal work) · *Study* (academic) |
| Short-form writing | **Field notes** | Working notes, Notes | *Blog* (forbidden, §35) · *Insights* (marketing) · *Musings* (unserious) |
| Professional work | **Professional work** | Engagements | *Client work* (implies he had clients — he was an intern on a team) · *Consulting* (false) |
| Built artifacts | **Tools** / **Builds** | Artifacts | *Products* (overclaim) · *Solutions* (marketing) |
| Research | **Open questions** | Research | *Research* alone implies published output he does not yet have. **Use only once something is published.** |
| Future direction | **Roadmap** | Direction, Intended work | *Vision* (corporate) · *Mission* (corporate) |
| Current focus | **Now** | Currently | *What I'm up to* (too casual) |
| Inactive work | **Archived** | Dormant, Set aside | *Deprecated* (implies it once had users) |
| Unfinished work | **Unresolved** / **In progress** | Open | *Coming soon* (a promise; often broken) · *WIP* (jargon) |
| Uncertainty | **What I couldn't determine** | Unresolved, Open questions | *Limitations* as a boxed label — see §3 |
| Findings | **What I concluded** | Conclusion | *Results* (implies measurement) · *Impact* (business-speak) |

## 2. Status vocabulary

Applies to investigations, tools, and roadmap items alike.

| Status | Meaning | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| **Closed** | Worked through; a conclusion was reached | The reasoning is published |
| **Open** | Actively being worked on | Visible recent activity |
| **Unresolved** | Investigated; no satisfactory conclusion reached | The reasoning *and* the dead end are published |
| **Intended** | Scoped, not started | A written scope only. **No conclusions permitted.** |
| **Archived** | No longer being pursued | A stated reason for stopping |

**Why this set beats the earlier proposals.** "Deployed / In development / Under investigation / Scoped" was assurance-register vocabulary that structurally favoured GRC. "Built / In progress / Research / Planned" was accurate but artifact-centred — and artifacts are exactly where Het is weakest.

**"Unresolved" is the important addition.** No portfolio in the entire Phase 1 corpus had a status meaning *"I looked into this and didn't get anywhere."* It is honest, it is unusual, and it is only credible if genuinely used.

## 3. Honesty vocabulary — how to avoid a "Limitations box"

Phase 1's strongest finding was that documented limitations increase credibility. Phase 2 refines the prescription, because a repeated boxed component would become decoration.

**Preferred: inline sentences in the practitioner's own voice.**

Good:
- *"It doesn't tell you whether a matching rule is correctly scoped."*
- *"I couldn't establish whether this was a misconfiguration or intentional."*
- *"This holds for the environments I tested. I don't know if it generalises."*

Avoid:
- ❌ A recurring **Limitations:** heading on every item
- ❌ *"Note: this project has limitations"* — content-free
- ❌ Apologetic framing — *"unfortunately I wasn't able to…"*

**Where it applies:**

| Content type | Required? |
|---|---|
| Investigation | **Yes** — "what I couldn't determine" is the natural closing move |
| Tool / build | **Yes** — per Het's own strategy document |
| Professional work | **Yes**, but at method level — what the approach could not cover, never a finding |
| Field note | **No** — it would be padding on a short piece |
| Roadmap item | **N/A** — an intention has no results to qualify |

## 4. Professional-work vocabulary

The correct register, per the Doyensec convention: **anonymised client, real methodological depth, silent omission of specifics.**

**Permitted:** *"a regulated financial institution"* · *"CSCRF and IRDAI audit engagements"* · *"control mapping"* · *"evidence collection"* · *"remediation documentation"* · *"scope"* · *"as part of a team"* · *"my role was…"*

**Forbidden:** any client identifier or narrowing detail · any finding, count, severity, or metric · internal systems, tooling, or architecture · dates precise enough to correlate · **any announcement of withholding** (*"I can't discuss…"*, *"details redacted"*). Omission is silent.

**Role precision is mandatory.** Het contributed to team engagements. *"I worked on"* and *"I assisted with"* are accurate; *"I conducted"* and *"I led"* are not, unless verified.

## 5. Words banned outright

**Marketing:** passionate · dedicated professional · proven track record · unwavering commitment · cutting-edge · leverage · synergy · results-driven · detail-oriented · problem solver · innovative · game-changing.

**False seniority:** expert · specialist · authority · veteran · seasoned · senior *(unqualified)* · architect · thought leader.

**Hacker persona:** ninja · guru · wizard · elite · ethical hacker *(as identity)* · "beyond legal" · SYSTEM ONLINE · ACCESS GRANTED · INITIALIZING.

**Unverifiable:** any percentage proficiency · any metric without a source · "10+ years/frameworks/tools" · star or download counts as capability.

**Structural:** *Services* (he seeks employment) · *Clients* (he had none of his own) · *Portfolio* as a section name (the site is the portfolio).
