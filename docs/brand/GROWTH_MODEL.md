# Growth Model

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 2)

**Requirement:** reject any identity that only works for a student. The brand must become *more* credible as Het grows, not obsolete.

---

| Direction | Today | +5 years | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Investigator** | Works — investigations exist from professional work already done | A body of investigations across domains is exactly what a senior practitioner's site looks like | ✅ **Ages upward** |

## Four horizons

### TODAY — minimal public evidence

**State:** ~7-month internship, CSCRF/IRDAI involvement, zero verified original repositories, no published writing.

**Brand behaviour:** professional work carries credibility; reasoning carries differentiation; the EMERGING tier is visibly empty and honestly so.

**The load-bearing question:** how many investigations can actually be described? **Still unanswered.** Below ~6, the brand promises more than the content delivers.

**Failure mode:** the site looks like a well-designed shell. Mitigation is content, not design.

### +1 YEAR — several substantial projects

**Expected:** GitHub remediated, one or two projects taken to product standard, several investigations published, possibly a first role.

**Brand behaviour:** artifacts move from Tier 3 to Tier 1 in the trust model. The EMERGING tier fills. Investigations begin to cluster by domain, and the domain axis starts earning its place in navigation.

**Structural change required: none.** An artifact is an output attached to an investigation — a data change, not an architecture change.

**Failure mode:** repository count becomes the story and the brand drifts toward artifact-dumping. Guarded against by never surfacing volume as a number.

### +3 YEARS — strong technical portfolio

**Expected:** a real body of work, some domains genuinely deep, professional experience beyond the first role.

**Brand behaviour:** the domain × status structure starts to matter — enough investigations exist for clustering to reveal expertise rather than advertise sparseness. Early investigations become **retrospective evidence of consistent judgment**, which is a compounding asset no new portfolio can replicate.

**Structural change required:** possibly a second navigation axis (chronology or depth). Not a redesign.

**Failure mode:** early modest work looks embarrassing against later work. **Mitigation: it shouldn't be hidden.** A visible progression from modest early reasoning to stronger later reasoning is more credible than a portfolio that appears to have begun fully formed — and it is only available to someone who started honestly.

### +5 YEARS — professional security-engineering career

**Expected:** senior-track practitioner, possibly published research, deep specialisation in some tracks.

**Brand behaviour:** unchanged in kind, greater in weight. "Here is how I work problems out" is what senior practitioners' sites say — Kettle's research portfolio and Miessler's thesis-led site are both, structurally, this.

**The vocabulary that must age:** "early-career practitioner" is stage-bound and will need replacing. **Everything else survives** — "investigation", "unresolved", "what I couldn't determine" are stage-neutral. This is a deliberate property of the vocabulary chosen in `VOCABULARY.md`.

## What must never change

Four commitments. Changing any of them breaks the brand's continuity:

1. **Reasoning is shown.** The core promise.
2. **Uncertainty is stated.** Its value increases with seniority — a senior engineer admitting limits is more impressive, not less.
3. **Claims stay proportionate to evidence.** The specific proportion changes; the discipline does not.
4. **Confidentiality is handled silently and correctly.** Becomes more important as engagements grow more sensitive.

## The compounding asset

Most portfolios are rebuilt every few years because they were snapshots. This one is designed to accumulate:

- Every investigation is **dated and permanent.**
- Early investigations are not embarrassments but **evidence of trajectory.**
- The archive itself becomes the credential — the mechanism behind Miessler's 27-year archive and tl;dr sec's 340+ issues.

**The corollary is a real constraint:** if the archive is to be an asset, entries cannot be quietly deleted when they become inconvenient. Corrections should be visible as corrections. That discipline costs something now and is worth a great deal at year five.

## Scenario resilience

| Scenario | Investigator brand |
|---|---|
| Het writes little (0–3/yr) | **Holds.** Investigations are retrospective, not scheduled |
| Het ships no projects for a year | **Holds, but strains.** The EMERGING tier stays empty and eventually reads as stalled |
| Het moves away from GRC | **Holds.** The unit is domain-neutral — this was the decisive advantage over The Register |
| Het moves away from VAPT | **Holds.** Same reason |
| Het goes to graduate school | **Holds.** Research becomes investigations |
| Het stops maintaining the site | Fails — as any evolving site would. Dated entries at least age honestly rather than misleading |

**The only scenario the brand does not survive is prolonged inactivity**, and no brand strategy solves that. It is a content problem, not a design one.
