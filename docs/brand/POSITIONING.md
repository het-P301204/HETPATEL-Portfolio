## 2. The honest shape of the advantage

Stated plainly, because the brand collapses if this is fudged.

Het's advantage is **access-derived, not skill-derived.**

He was placed inside real regulated engagements — CSCRF and IRDAI audits at a financial firm — at a career stage when almost nobody gets that. That is genuinely scarce: a student can build a Splunk lab at home, but cannot manufacture exposure to a regulated audit.

**What that entitles him to claim:** having seen how regulated assurance work is actually conducted, and having formed judgment from it.

**What it does not entitle him to claim:** expertise, seniority, or authority. Seven months of exposure is exposure.

The brand must sit exactly on that line. Cross it and the strongest asset becomes the biggest liability, because overclaiming is the fastest way to lose a technically strong reader — a point both the research and Het's own strategy document make independently.

## 4. Positioning statement — PROPOSED

> **Het Patel is an early-career security practitioner working between technical testing and regulated assurance.**
>
> He has tested live web applications and worked on CSCRF and IRDAI audits for a financial firm — which means he has seen how findings become obligations, and how obligations become evidence.
>
> He publishes how he reasons through security problems, including what he could not determine.

Three notes on the wording:
- *"Early-career practitioner"* — a stage, not a rank. No seniority implied.
- *"has seen how"* — carefully chosen. Claims perspective, not mastery.
- The third sentence is the differentiator and the promise in one line.

## 5. What is deliberately not claimed

Senior engineer · architect · expert · specialist · authority · researcher (until research is published) · consultant · "10+ frameworks" · any metric that cannot be verified · AWS/Azure security capability · production Splunk experience · authorship of the roadmap or strategy frameworks.
