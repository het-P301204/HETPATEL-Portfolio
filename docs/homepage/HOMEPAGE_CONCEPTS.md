# Homepage Concepts

**Date:** 2026-08-15 (Phase 5E) · Six information-architecture directions. **No visual design.**

Phase 4 explored five *opening experiences* (`../design/HOMEPAGE_EXPERIENCE.md`) and selected H1. These are different: they are **information architectures**, they are scored against constraint T9 which did not exist in Phase 4, and they assume the 5D measurement that an investigation excerpt reads as editorial publication.

D below is Phase 4's selection carried forward unchanged, so the comparison is honest.

---

## A — WORK-FIRST

**Sequence:** selected work → identity → experience → contact.

The page opens with the work itself. Identity is a line beneath it. The argument: let the work speak; the person is inferred.

**Reads as:** a body of work. **Identity:** by inference.

**Strength.** Fastest route to substance for a practitioner. Scales beautifully — at 25 investigations this is the natural shape.

**Weakness — decisive at launch.** Het has zero verified built work and three investigations. Work-first with three items reads as a site that has run out of things to say. It is also the Phase 1 austerity trap: *"reads as confident from a known name, unfinished from an unknown one."* And it repeats the exact structural error that made the investigation page read as a publication — content before person.

---

## B — PLAIN PROFESSIONAL STATEMENT

**Sequence:** identity paragraph → list of work → contact.

Name, then two or three sentences of what he does and where. Then a list. Nothing else.

**Reads as:** portfolio, unambiguously. **Identity:** immediate and explicit.

**Strength.** Perfect recruiter clarity. Works at any volume. Impossible to misread. Directly satisfies T9.

**Weakness.** This is Phase 4's H3, which scored 53 and was described as *"the least memorable — a visitor could leave without encountering any reasoning."* That judgment stands. It converts the site into a competent résumé page and discards the only thing that makes it different. It is what `bugatsec.dev` is, and Phase 1 called that the most important cautionary reference in the corpus.

**Kept from it:** the identity paragraph itself. B is not wrong; it is incomplete.

---

## C — ENGAGEMENT-FIRST

**Sequence:** the regulated-assurance context → identity → work → contact.

Opens on the strongest verified asset: professional work inside CSCRF and IRDAI audit engagements.

**Reads as:** portfolio, leaning consultancy. **Identity:** through the work's context.

**Strength.** Leads with the scarcest thing Het has. Immediately separates him from the SOC-lab archetype, which is the crowded ground.

**Weakness.** Two, and both are serious. It leads with an asset that is **one internship deep** — front-loading it invites the reader to ask "and what else?" before anything else has been established. And an engagement-led opening is the structure of a consultancy site, which fails the portfolio test in a different direction: it makes the *work* the subject and the person the vendor. It also strains hardest against the confidentiality boundary, because the most prominent block on the page becomes the one with the least sayable detail.

---

## D — INVESTIGATION-FIRST *(Phase 4 H1, as specified)*

**Sequence:** identity line → featured investigation at ~45% → index preview → notes → contact.

**Reads as:** editorial publication. **This is now measured, not predicted.**

**Strength.** Genuinely the fastest route to the brand promise. A visitor who reads only the homepage has *seen* reasoning and an admitted limit. That insight was right and survives into the selection.

**Weakness — disqualifying under T9.** 5D measured the investigation page's five-second impression as editorial publication. A homepage that gives 45% of its area to a full investigation excerpt — title, apparatus, question, opening reasoning — inherits that impression wholesale and becomes a publication with a bio line on top. It also gives professional experience no position at all, inverting the project's own evidence ranking.

**Kept from it:** the requirement that one real piece of reasoning and one real admitted limit appear on the homepage.

---

## E — THE STANDING RECORD

**Sequence:** identity → what the professional work actually was → selected work → one investigation in depth → what's next → contact.

A structured professional record. Identity states the lane. Experience is presented at **method level** — the kind of work, the role precision, the technical/assurance intersection — not as a resume block. Selected work is a small set of items carrying status and origin. One investigation gets enough space to show real reasoning and one admitted limit, but as an **evidenced item within a portfolio**, not as an article opening the page.

**Reads as:** portfolio, with editorial quality. **Identity:** stated, then evidenced, then demonstrated.

**Strength.** It is the only direction that satisfies T9 *and* preserves D's insight. It puts the strongest verified asset in the second position, where the evidence ranking says it belongs. It degrades correctly — at one investigation it is identity + experience + one investigation + contact, and that is a complete page. It grows into Kettle's thematic grouping without redesign.

**Weakness.** More positions than B or D, so it risks reading as a conventional multi-section portfolio if the sections are labelled conventionally. **The mitigation is discipline, not layout:** no section is named after a resume heading, nothing appears that has no content, and the total is short.

---

## F — CLAIM AND EVIDENCE

**Sequence:** identity → capability claims, each bound inline to the thing that evidences it → contact.

The organising idea: never state a capability without binding it, in the same line, to the engagement or investigation that evidences it. A capability with no evidence is not listed.

**Reads as:** portfolio. **Identity:** through demonstrable capability.

**Strength.** Structurally elegant, and it solves two problems at once. The unfalsifiable-skills problem disappears because nothing can be claimed without a pointer. The "where is the evidence" question is answered in the same sentence as the claim. It is the exact inverse of a skills grid, which makes it strongly anti-generic. It also self-manages empty states at line level.

**Weakness.** It needs evidence to bind to, and Het currently has one engagement and three investigations — so the same two or three items would be cited repeatedly, which exposes the thinness rather than hiding it. It also risks reading as a matrix, and a matrix of claims is uncomfortably close to the skills table it is trying to replace. It defers the person: capability-first is still not quite person-first.

**Kept from it:** **the binding principle.** No capability claim appears anywhere on the homepage unless the thing that evidences it is named in the same sentence. This is adopted as a rule regardless of which architecture wins.

## Summary

| | Opens with | Reads as | Fails at low volume | T9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A Work-first | the work | body of work | **yes** | ⚠️ |
| B Plain statement | the person | portfolio | no | ✅ |
| C Engagement-first | the engagement | consultancy | no | ⚠️ |
| D Investigation-first | an investigation | **publication** | no | ❌ |
| **E Standing record** | the person | **portfolio** | no | ✅ |
| F Claim and evidence | capability | portfolio | **yes** | ✅ |
