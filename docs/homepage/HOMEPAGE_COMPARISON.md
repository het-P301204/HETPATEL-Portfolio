## Why the scores fall where they do

**Portfolio clarity.** B scores 10 because it is nothing but a professional statement — it cannot be misread. D scores 2 because 5D *measured* the failure; this is the only cell in the table backed by a rendered result rather than judgment. E scores 9 rather than 10 because it carries more positions than B, and every additional position is a chance to drift editorial.

**Zero-content resilience.** The decisive column. A scores 2 — work-first with three items advertises the gap. F scores 3 for the same reason: the binding principle needs things to bind to, and citing the same engagement four times exposes thinness rather than concealing it. B and E score 9–10 because both remain complete when everything below the identity block collapses.

**Distinctiveness.** D genuinely wins this on its own terms — nothing in the corpus opens with someone's unresolved reasoning. E gives some of that up by moving the investigation down the page. **That loss is real and is the main cost of the selection.** It is accepted because distinctiveness that is only legible after five minutes does not survive a reader who leaves at five seconds.

**Personality.** B's 3 is the important number. It is `bugatsec.dev` — competent, corporate, voice-free — which Phase 1 identified as *"intentional genericization through absence of personality markers"* and named the most important cautionary reference in the corpus. B is safe and it is the failure mode.

**Mobile quality.** F scores 5 because claim-plus-evidence pairs want two columns and collapse into an ambiguous list at 375px. E scores 8 because every position is a single prose column that stacks without reflow.

**Investigation integration.** D is 10 by construction. E is 9 because the investigation is still present in full voice, with real reasoning and a real admitted limit — just not first.

---

## The two-way fight

The realistic contest is **B vs E**, because both satisfy T9 and both survive at launch volume. A, C, D and F each fail at least one non-negotiable.

**B's case:** maximum clarity, zero risk, works at any volume, impossible to misread. Phase 4 already assessed this shape and scored it 53/70 — the lowest of five.

**E's case:** it is B plus the two things B discards — the professional work at method level, and one piece of visible reasoning with an admitted limit.

**The deciding argument.** B satisfies the constraint by removing everything that could violate it. That is not a design; it is an absence of one. It would produce a page a recruiter understands and forgets — and the brief's own target is not "understood" but *"interesting person, let's investigate further."* The 5-minute memory test from Phase 3 still governs: if a visitor cannot complete *"the interesting thing about that site was…"*, the homepage has failed regardless of how clear it was.

E keeps the clarity that satisfies T9 and adds the evidence and the reasoning that make the clarity worth having.

---

## Selected: E — THE STANDING RECORD

With three borrowings, each recorded so its origin is traceable:

| Borrowed | From | What is taken |
|---|---|---|
| The identity paragraph | **B** | Two or three plain sentences: who, what, where, at what stage. Complete without scrolling at 375px |
| One real limit on the homepage | **D** | A visitor who reads only the homepage must still have seen genuine reasoning and something Het could not determine |
| The binding principle | **F** | **No capability claim appears without the thing that evidences it named in the same sentence.** Adopted as a standing rule |

And two explicit rejections:

- **A's work-first opening** — reserved. It becomes correct somewhere past 15 investigations, and E grows into it without redesign.
- **C's engagement-led opening** — rejected permanently, not deferred. Leading with the engagement makes the work the subject and the person the vendor, which is a consultancy, and it puts the most prominent block on the page where the least is sayable.

**Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH**, pending the rendered tests. The residual risk is E's own weakness — that six positions read as a conventional multi-section portfolio. The tests that would catch it are the portfolio test, the genericity test and the five-second impression.
