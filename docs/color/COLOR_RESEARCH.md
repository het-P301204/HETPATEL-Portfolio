## What "warm editorial neutral" must not become

| Failure | Looks like | Guard |
|---|---|---|
| **Beige design trend** | Sand/oat backgrounds, muddy brown text, terracotta accents | Ground stays very close to white — a warm *tint*, not a colour. Text is near-black, not brown |
| **Lifestyle editorial** | Cream + blush + generous serif | No pastel; a sober accent |
| **Luxury agency** | Off-white + gold + thin type | No metallic; no hairline type |
| **Academic paper** | Pure white + black + Times | Warm ground and a contemporary serif |
| **Corporate** | Grey-blue + safe blue accent | Warmth in the ground; accent used at ~2% only |

**The operative distinction: a warm ground is a *near-white with a trace of warmth*, not a beige.** If the background reads as "a colour" rather than "paper", it has gone too far.

---

## Why warm rather than pure white or dark

**Against dark:** it is the category signature, measured on every cybersecurity site inspected. Adopting it concedes the single clearest differentiation available for nothing.

**Against pure white:** `#FFFFFF` with near-black text is the highest-contrast and most clinical option. It reads as documentation or as an academic PDF, and it makes long-form serif reading harsher than necessary.

**For warm near-white:** it reads as paper, supports sustained reading, and is essentially absent from the security category. It costs no credibility — printed technical reports have been set on warm stock for a century.

## Accent hue — the reasoning

Constraint from Phase 5A: **not cool-cyber** (cyan, electric blue, neon green, purple).

| Candidate | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep ink blue** | Reads as document ink; calm; excellent contrast; unambiguously not "cyber" | Blue links are the web default — low distinctiveness |
| Oxblood / madder | Distinctive; editorial | Pushes literary/academic — the F4 risk |
| Deep teal | Distinctive; technical | Drifts toward the cool-cyber family |
| Warm grey (no accent) | Maximum restraint | Leaves links with no affordance beyond underline |

**Recommendation: deep ink blue.** The reasoning is that the accent's main job is *links*, and a link should be legible and unremarkable rather than expressive. Distinctiveness is carried by typography and the apparatus, not by hue — consistent with the approved position that the visual system is *diagnostic*, not unique.

**Oxblood recorded as the documented alternate**, to be compared in 5D. This is a judgment call, and it is flagged as one.

---

## Colour must carry no meaning

Established across the system and not revisited: status is a word, links are underlined, the register split is typographic.

**Consequence: the palette is small by construction**, because there is nothing for a larger one to encode.
