## 1 investigation

**Pages:** Home · the investigation · About. **No index page** — a list of one is not a list.

**Homepage:** identity → featured investigation (full) → contact. Three blocks, ends.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Intentionally small? | Yes — three complete pages |
| Whitespace excessive? | No, provided pages end where content ends |
| Architecture asking for absent content? | **No** — index and preview simply do not exist |
| Weakest aspect | The apparatus has nothing to compare against. `origin` and `status` are facts about one item, not a pattern |

**Recommendation:** at n=1 present as a single-page statement rather than a portfolio. The architecture permits both.

## 3 investigations — recommended launch minimum

Index appears. Homepage carries two preview rows. Navigation reaches four items.

**The first volume where the apparatus does comparative work** — one `open` against two `closed`, or one `professional` against two `self-initiated`, is information a reader can actually use.

Density: tight index, tight preview. **A tight index of three reads as complete; a spacious one reads as a placeholder for thirty.**

## What makes a small portfolio look intentional

Five mechanisms, in order of effect:

1. **Sections disappear rather than emptying.** No "coming soon", no placeholders, no empty regions.
2. **The head apparatus gives every investigation substance.** A short piece with four real fields reads as a complete record.
3. **Density where density is honest.** Tight index, tight preview.
4. **Pages end where content ends.** No full-viewport sections forcing scroll.
5. **Navigation grows with content.** Two or three items at n=1 is honest; four items with one empty is not.

---

## Where the layout is weakest

**At n=1**, and the reason is structural rather than spatial: the apparatus's value is comparative, and one item compares to nothing.

**Irreducible.** Mitigated by launching at three, and by the single-page fallback below that.
