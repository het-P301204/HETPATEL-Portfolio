**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 5B)

The question is the conceptual entry point — what needed working out.

## Recommended: C — the question is the first paragraph

**Written in the voice, not set as a device.**

> *"A test environment had an allow-list that behaved correctly in review and permitted unexpected requests in practice. I wanted to know where the gap came from."*

That is the question, and it is also the first paragraph of the reasoning. It needs no special treatment.

---

## Why the more "designed" options lose

**A and D score highest on importance and lowest on flow — and flow is what this site is for.** Setting the question larger or in its own region turns it into a pull quote: a designed object the reader consumes and then leaves, before starting the article properly. It also creates a second opening, so the piece begins twice.

**Both fail badly on short investigations.** At 150 words, a monumentalised question followed by three paragraphs is top-heavy — the framing outweighs the content.

**B is the conventional editorial deck**, and it duplicates the title's job.

---

## Geometry

- No special size, weight, or colour. **L3 body prose, identical to what follows.**
- OPEN space above (separating it from the head apparatus), NORMAL below.
- Full reading measure.
- No quotation marks, no italics, no indentation.
- **Not literally interrogative.** A question may be stated as a situation; forcing every opening into "Why does X…?" would become a visible template.

---

## Why this is defensible despite scoring lower on importance

The question's importance is carried by **position** — it is the first thing after the title and apparatus, so it cannot be missed — not by treatment.

This is the same principle as `UNCERTAINTY_SYSTEM.md`: **position over decoration.** Applying it consistently at both the opening and the closing movement is what makes the document read as continuous rather than as a series of designed components.
