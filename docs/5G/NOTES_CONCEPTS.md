# Notes — Concepts

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Not visually validated.**

Five directions for how short-form thinking gets a home. C is `NOTES_LAYOUT.md` (5B) carried forward
unchanged so the comparison is honest.

The failure to beat is structural: **a dated, reverse-chronological, short-form, recurring format
is a blog** unless something specific stops it being one.

---

## A — THE STREAM

Notes rendered inline, newest first, date above each, nothing else. No titles.

**Reads as:** a running record. **Strength:** lowest possible friction — a two-sentence note costs
nothing to publish, which is what keeps the archive accruing under `FIELD_NOTES_MODEL.md`'s
low-volume Scenario A. No titles means no pressure to make each note sound significant.

**Weakness:** without titles, nothing is scannable and nothing is linkable by name. At 30 notes a
reader cannot find the one they remember. It is also the format closest to a microblog.

---

## B — TITLED ENTRIES

Each note gets a title, a date, and a body. Index lists titles; notes have their own pages.

**Reads as:** a blog. **Decisively.**

This is the direction that must be named and rejected explicitly, because it is what everyone
builds. A title plus a date plus a body plus a click *is* a blog post, whatever it is called, and
`FIELD_NOTES_MODEL.md` §2 gives the substantive reason it fails here: **a blog is a promise of
cadence**, and Het's writing capacity is unverified. Three posts and no fourth reads as abandonment.

**Rejected.** Kept from it: nothing.

---

## C — INLINE INDEX *(Phase 5B, as specified)*

Notes render inline in their index. Date above, body beneath, OPEN between. Permalink on the date.
Notes over ~400 words get their own page with the index showing the first paragraph.

**Reads as:** a record of observations.

**Strength.** The key insight is 5B's and it is right: *requiring a click to read forty words is
friction with no payoff.* **The index is the content.** That single decision removes the blog's
central mechanic — there is no "post" to click into, so there is nothing to be a feed of.

It also solves the title problem A has: a note can carry a first sentence that functions as its own
title without the ceremony of one.

**Weakness.** Scannability at volume — the same problem as A, deferred rather than solved. At 30+
notes an inline index is a long page with no anchors.

---

## D — NOTES AS MARGINALIA

Notes attach to investigations and appear alongside them, with no standalone index.

**Reads as:** apparatus. **Strength:** structurally elegant — a note becomes context for the work it
came from, and the relationship is visible rather than asserted.

**Weakness — decisive.** `FIELD_NOTES_MODEL.md` §4 states most notes will be **standalone**, and
that is the expected case. A model that requires a parent investigation would either suppress most
notes or force false attachments. It also destroys the type's main purpose: giving small work an
honest home *of its own*.

---

## E — GROUPED OBSERVATIONS

Notes grouped by theme rather than by date.

**Reads as:** a knowledge base. **Strength:** the anti-feed structure, taken furthest. Grouping is
what 5F found converts a list into an argument.

**Weakness.** Wrong for this type. `FIELD_NOTES_MODEL.md` §6 is explicit that **reverse chronological
is correct for notes — the one place on the site where it is** — because a note is an observation at
a point in time, and recency is genuinely its most relevant attribute. Grouping also implies
completeness of coverage that a handful of observations does not have.

**Kept:** a year separator at 25+, which `NOTES_LAYOUT.md` already anticipates.

---

## Comparison

| | Reads as | Scannable at 30 | Blog risk | Works at 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A Stream | running record | ❌ | medium | ✅ |
| B Titled entries | **a blog** | ✅ | **fatal** | ✅ |
| **C Inline index (5B)** | **record of observations** | ⚠️ | **low** | ✅ |
| D Marginalia | apparatus | n/a | none | ❌ |
| E Grouped | knowledge base | ✅ | none | ❌ |

**Selected: C**, unchanged from 5B, with E's year separator retained as the volume answer.

The reason C wins is one structural property: **the index is the content, so there is no post to
click into.** Every other anti-blog measure is cosmetic next to that.
