# Art Direction Options — five directions

**Date:** 2026-08-15 · **Status: defined, not yet built.** Nothing here has been rendered. When
these are prototyped, this document gets replaced by what was actually seen.

Each direction is constrained by the same hard rules: **zero containers, zero mono region-labels,
zero centred-column-only composition, composition continues below the fold, and the material is
Het's real facts** — VAPT, Info eShield, CSCRF, IRDAI, ISO 27001, investigations, reasoning,
attribution ceiling.

---

## The design problem, stated properly

Het has **no imagery, no logo, no product screenshots, and three investigations**. Every reference
that wins on visual impact wins with a *field* — an illustration, a 3D object, a saturated colour
plane. Het's field can only be built from **typography, colour and geometry.**

That constraint is the brief. Five answers to it follow.

---

## DIRECTION A · OVERFLOW

**Core idea.** The viewport is a crop of something larger. Type does not fit, and that is the point.

**Visual language.** `HET PATEL` set at 300–400px in a heavy grotesk, running off the right edge —
the viewport shows `HET PATE`. Below it, the stance sentence at 22px flush left at the same origin.
A single saturated field colour, edge to edge, not black.

**Hero.** Statement small top-left, wordmark oversized and clipped. Reading order by position.

**Work.** Investigation titles at 48–72px, each **starting at a different left offset** so the index
descends diagonally. Hover moves the title *left toward the margin* and brings the framing sentence
in beneath it. No rows, no rules, no backgrounds.

**Experience.** Info eShield set at wordmark scale as its own moment — the employer name treated as
typographically important, with the honest caveat set small directly beneath it.

**Motion.** Entrance: the wordmark assembles from the right, the clipped letters arriving last.
Hover: tracking tightens and the title shifts left. Scroll: the alignment axis migrates.

**Strength.** The most confident and the most memorable. Directly answers the 20-portfolio test.
**Weakness.** Clipped type is a strong claim; if the rest of the page is quiet it looks like a
mistake. Mobile needs a different clipping logic or it becomes unreadable.
**Genericity risk.** Low — the specific crop of *HET PATEL* is not transferable to another name.

## DIRECTION B · PLUMB LINE

**Core idea.** A single vertical rule runs the full height of the site. Everything hangs off it at
varying indents, like a plumb line or a measuring datum.

**Visual language.** One hairline, fixed, at roughly 22% from the left. Content attaches at
different distances — some flush to it, some far right, some crossing it. Warm off-white ground,
one ink, one accent. Drafting-grid intersection marks where sections meet the line.

**Hero.** The name crosses the line: `HET` left of it, `PATEL` right of it. The stance sentence
hangs from the line below.

**Work.** Each investigation attaches to the line at a different indent. Depth of indent encodes
something real — professional vs self-initiated, or open vs closed. Hover extends a short rule from
the plumb line to the title.

**Experience.** Attaches at the deepest indent, marking it as the most anchored fact on the page.

**Motion.** Entrance: the line draws top to bottom, then content attaches in sequence. Scroll: a
marker travels the line showing position.

**Strength.** Architectural, precise, and the technical register comes from *drawing* rather than
from software. Scales indefinitely — new work is another attachment. Distinctive without decoration.
**Weakness.** A vertical rule is one step from a sidebar; it must never acquire a background.
**Genericity risk.** Low-medium. The structure is distinctive; the indent encoding is what makes it
Het's.

---

## DIRECTION C · FIELD & FIGURE

**Core idea.** A saturated colour field owns the viewport. Identity sits small at the edges. The
work is the only thing allowed to be large.

**Visual language.** One committed non-category colour — deep oxblood, ink indigo or slate green;
explicitly not black, not grey, not security blue. Name at 20px top-left, nav top-right, both quiet.
The field is the impact.

**Hero.** Almost empty by design, but *anchored*: the stance sentence pinned to the bottom-left
corner at 40px, the field above it. Emptiness as tension, per the edge-anchoring pattern.

**Work.** Full-bleed. One investigation title at a time at enormous scale as the reader scrolls, the
field shifting hue slightly per investigation. The index becomes a sequence rather than a list.

**Experience.** A field inversion — ground and ink swap for that one section.

**Motion.** Entrance: the field floods in, then the identity fades at the edges. Scroll: field
colour transitions between investigations.

**Strength.** Most "expensive." The colour becomes the memory hook, which no other direction has.
**Weakness.** One investigation per screen is slow and works against the recruiter's 20 seconds.
Colour-driven identity is the hardest thing to get right and the easiest to get wrong.
**Genericity risk.** **High** — a coloured field with small identity type describes a great many
sites. This is the direction most likely to fail the retest.

---

## DIRECTION D · MARGINALIA

**Core idea.** A reading column with a genuinely active margin. Not a sidebar — an annotation layer
that behaves like a scholar's marginal notes.

**Visual language.** Prose in a comfortable measure, offset left of centre. The wide right margin
carries dates, status, revisions, cross-references — set small, aligned to the line they annotate,
never boxed. Paper ground, ink, one red for revision.

**Hero.** The stance sentence at 56px in the main column; the margin carries the role and employer
as an annotation, aligned to the exact line they qualify.

**Work.** A continuous prose index — Het's own sentences about each investigation — with titles set
large inline. The margin carries the record. Hover highlights the annotation-to-line relationship.

**Experience.** Prose in the column; the attribution ceiling in the margin, which is exactly what a
margin is for.

**Motion.** Entrance: column first, margin annotations arrive after, staggered. Hover: a hairline
connects annotation to line.

**Strength.** The only direction where the *revision register* — the site's most distinctive asset —
becomes the visual system rather than a component. Deeply personal, scales beautifully with writing.
**Weakness.** **Closest to the rejected editorial register.** The margin must be genuinely active or
it is a sidebar. Hard on mobile, where margins collapse.
**Genericity risk.** Low — it depends on Het having annotations, which requires real revision
practice.

---

## DIRECTION E · STATE

**Core idea.** The site is a record of things whose state has changed. Composition encodes state
change: open work sits differently on the page from closed work.

**Visual language.** Position and alignment carry meaning. Open investigations sit further left and
larger; closed ones sit further right and smaller. Revised ones show both positions with the earlier
one struck through in the margin. Near-monochrome with one colour reserved for change.

**Hero.** The stance sentence, with one word visibly *revised* — the earlier word struck, the
current one beside it. The identity idea and the epistemics are the same gesture.

**Work.** A composition where the position of each item is data. Nothing is a row.

**Experience.** Positioned as the most-settled item on the page.

**Motion.** Entrance: items animate from their previous state into their current one. Hover on a
revised item reveals what it used to say.

**Strength.** The most *conceptually* original and the most specific to Het — it is his epistemic
honesty made into a visual system. Impossible to transfer to a designer's portfolio.
**Weakness.** The most likely to be illegible. Position-as-data must be learnable in seconds or it
is a puzzle. Highest execution risk of the five.
**Genericity risk.** **Lowest of all five.** It cannot describe anyone whose work does not get
revised.

## Which three to build

| | Impact | Identity | Memorability | Hiring | Originality | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A · Overflow** | 9 | 9 | 9 | 7 | 8 | Medium |
| **B · Plumb Line** | 7 | 8 | 7 | 8 | 8 | **Low** |
| **C · Field & Figure** | 9 | 5 | 8 | 5 | 5 | High |
| **D · Marginalia** | 6 | 8 | 6 | 8 | 7 | Medium |
| **E · State** | 7 | 9 | 8 | 7 | **10** | **High** |

**Build A, B and E.**

- **A** for maximum visual impact and the memorability test.
- **B** because it is the most likely to survive contact with real content at every viewport, and
  because architectural precision is the right register for regulated assurance work.
- **E** because it is the only one that could not be anyone else's website, and the brief's
  hardest requirement is that Het's material activate unique visual relationships.

**C is not built.** It scores well on impact and badly on everything the brief actually cares
about — it is "premium" in the shallow sense and would fail the genericity retest.

**D is not built as a system**, but its margin-as-annotation mechanism should be tested inside B,
where a plumb line and an active margin are closely related ideas.
