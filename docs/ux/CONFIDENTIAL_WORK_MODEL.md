# Confidential Work Model

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 3)

How CSCRF, IRDAI and internship work appear without exposing anything confidential — and without making confidentiality a device.

---

## 1. The governing principle

Phase 1 established this from five independent sources and it is not revisited:

> **Silent omission plus visible method.**

Doyensec anonymises a client (*"a Communication Platform as a Service"*) while publishing specific Unicode codepoints and regex flaws — and **never announces what it withheld.** Include Security publishes no case studies at all and never explains why.

**Therefore banned outright:**
- ❌ "[REDACTED]" styling as a visual device
- ❌ "Details omitted under NDA"
- ❌ Blurred or censored imagery
- ❌ A "Confidential Work" section label
- ❌ Any lock, shield, or classification-marking iconography
- ❌ Any sentence drawing attention to the boundary

To a sector-literate reader — which audience 2 is, by profession — announcing discretion signals inexperience. Discretion demonstrates itself by being unremarked.

---

## 2. The structural solution

Established in `CONTENT_MODEL.md` §4 and it is the key move:

> **The engagement is context. The question Het worked out inside it is the investigation.**

This solves three problems at once:

1. **Honesty.** Het assisted on audits as an intern; he did not scope or lead them. Publishing "my CSCRF engagement" overstates his role. Publishing "how I worked out mapping a control to evidence an auditor would accept" is exactly true.
2. **Confidentiality.** The unit of publication becomes *his reasoning*, which he owns, rather than *the client's engagement*, which he does not. Most confidentiality risk disappears because the client's specifics were never the subject.
3. **Substance.** A summarised engagement is thin and generic. A reasoning problem is specific and interesting — and it is the brand promise.

---

## 3. What may and may not appear

| Publishable | Never |
|---|---|
| Role, at method level ("I worked on control mapping and evidence collection") | Client identity, or detail narrow enough to identify |
| Framework names — CSCRF, IRDAI, ISO 27001 | Any finding, count, severity, or metric |
| General domain and scope boundaries | Internal systems, architecture, tooling |
| Methodology and standards followed | Evidence artefacts, screenshots, documents |
| The reasoning Het personally applied | Dates precise enough to correlate |
| Lessons and general conclusions | Remediation status or timelines |
| Employer name (on the resume already) | Employer named *in connection with* a specific engagement |

**Role precision is mandatory.** "I worked on" and "I assisted with" are accurate. "I conducted" and "I led" are not.

## 4. Making it feel substantial without evidence

The concern: professional investigations have no artifacts, so will they feel weaker than ones that do?

**Four mechanisms, in order of strength:**

**1. Specificity of the reasoning.** The reasoning can be *completely* specific even when the context is generic. "I worked out that evidence which satisfies one framework often fails another because the two define the control boundary differently" is concrete, technical, and discloses nothing.

**2. The problem is genuinely harder.** Regulated assurance problems are more interesting than lab exercises. A well-chosen professional question outclasses a tool writeup on substance alone.

**3. Origin metadata does the work silently.** An investigation marked `professional` carries a different weight from one marked `self-initiated` — without a word of explanation, and without drawing attention to what is absent.

**4. The absent artifact is simply absent.** No placeholder, no "artifact unavailable", no empty slot. `ARTIFACT_MODEL.md` §5 covers this: an investigation without an artifact shows nothing where an artifact would be. Silence is the design.

## 5. Review gate — non-negotiable

Every professional investigation must be reviewed by Het before publication. **I cannot assess his legal obligations.**

**Pre-publication checks:**
1. Could a reader identify the client from any combination of details?
2. Does any sentence reveal a finding, weakness, or control state?
3. Is any date precise enough to correlate with a known engagement?
4. Is the stated role exactly accurate?
5. Would Het be comfortable if his employer read this?
6. Does any sentence draw attention to what is withheld? *(If yes, delete it.)*

**Check 1 is the subtle one.** Individually harmless details — sector, size, timing, framework combination — can be jointly identifying. The test is the combination, not each detail.

---

## 6. Volume expectation, stated plainly

Between roughly **two and five** professional investigations seem realistic from a seven-month internship. That is a reasonable core, and it is not a body of work on its own.

**Consequence:** professional investigations should be the *strongest* items but not the *only* items. Self-initiated and academic investigations must carry volume. If professional work is all there is, the site reads as a very short engagement history — which is what it would be.

This remains dependent on the still-unanswered question of how many investigations Het can actually describe.
