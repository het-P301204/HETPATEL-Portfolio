# Density and Whitespace

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 5A)

**The target:** SPARE, not EMPTY. The difference is whether the space is doing work.

---

## Density by surface

| Surface | Density | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Investigation detail** | **Open** | Sustained reading of 400–2,000 words |
| **Masthead apparatus** | **Dense** | Compact label/value pairs. This density is the system's visual signature |
| **Investigation index** | **Tight** | Scanned, not read |
| **Notes index** | **Tight–moderate** | Short content sits close together |
| **Homepage** | Open at top, tightening downward | Orientation needs air; the index below does not |
| **About** | Moderate | Reference material |
| **Artifacts** | Tight | A short list of links |

**The most important line in this document:** *density is not the opposite of quality.* The index should be dense, and the masthead should be dense. Editorial whitespace belongs around prose, not around metadata.

A common failure is applying uniform generosity everywhere — which makes an eight-item list occupy three screens and reads as an attempt to fill a page.

---

## What whitespace does here

| Function | Where |
|---|---|
| **Pacing** | Between movements — the reader is given a beat before the argument turns |
| **Hierarchy** | Larger gaps signal larger structural jumps. This is the primary structural tool |
| **Separation** | Replaces rules almost everywhere |
| **Reading comfort** | Line height and measure in the prose column |
| **Emphasis** | Rarely. Space around a single element is the only permitted non-typographic emphasis |

**Not for:** luxury, prestige, or "premium" feel. Space that communicates expense rather than structure is the design-portfolio failure mode named in `VISUAL_NORTH_STAR.md`.

## The low-content problem

Het may launch with three investigations. The system must read as *deliberately small* rather than *unfinished*.

**Five mechanisms, in order of importance:**

**1. Sections disappear rather than emptying.** No "coming soon", no placeholders, no empty shelves. Established in `../ux/EMPTY_STATE_AND_GROWTH.md` (P3-006) and it is the single most important rule here.

**2. The masthead gives every investigation substance.** A short investigation with a full masthead — status, date, domain, origin — reads as a complete record. Without it, a 150-word piece on an open page would look like a fragment. **This is a direct argument for Model C over a side column**: the masthead adds density exactly where content is thinnest.

**3. Density where density is honest.** A tight index of three items looks intentional. A spacious index of three items looks like a placeholder for thirty.

**4. Whitespace scales with the page, not with the viewport.** A short page is short. It does not stretch to fill the screen, and there is no full-viewport section forcing scroll on a page with little to scroll through.

**5. Craft carries the impression.** With near-zero imagery and motion, typographic quality is the entire visual argument. **Three well-set investigations look considered; three default-styled ones look abandoned.**

## Confidence with limited content

The site should behave like a small, well-kept publication rather than a large, mostly-empty one.

| Signals confidence | Signals emptiness |
|---|---|
| Three items, tightly set, complete | Three items spread across a long page |
| Homepage ending after the featured investigation | Homepage with four half-filled sections |
| No index page until there are ~3 items | An index page listing one item |
| Metadata present and exact | Metadata omitted because it looks sparse |
| Short pages | Padded pages |

**The failure to avoid:** a large empty frame. Generous margins around a nearly-blank page do not read as restraint — they read as a template awaiting content.

---

## Density and growth

| Volume | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| 1–3 investigations | Tightest overall. No index page. Homepage is short |
| 5–10 | Index appears; rhythm unchanged |
| 25 | Index density becomes an asset — a long, scannable list reads as a body of work |
| 50+ | Unchanged; filtering handles retrieval |

**Notably, the density scale does not change as content grows.** What changes is how much of it there is. A system tuned for three items that must be retuned at twenty-five would fail the growth requirement.

## Test

> **Does any whitespace on this page exist because there is nothing to put there?**

If yes, the page is too large, not too empty. **Shrink the frame; do not add content to fill it.**
