# Spatial Philosophy

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 5A) · Relationships, not values. Values in 5B.

---

## The governing relationship

```
READING COLUMN   >   apparatus   >   chrome
```

Space is allocated in proportion to how much thinking an element contains. Prose gets the most because it carries the argument; the apparatus gets enough to be dense and legible; navigation and footer get the least.

---

## Widths

| Element | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Reading measure** | The controlling dimension. Set for sustained reading of 800–2,000 words — comfortably narrower than most portfolio body text |
| **Content width** | Equal to the reading measure on investigations. There is no wider container for prose to sit inside |
| **Masthead apparatus** | **Same width as the reading column**, not narrower. It is a band across the top of the content, not a column beside it |
| **Inline insets** | Same left edge, marginally narrower right edge if any distinction is needed at all |
| **Index** | May exceed the reading measure — it is scanned, not read |
| **Outer margins** | Generous at large widths; the reading column does not grow to fill the viewport |

**Consequence:** on a wide screen there is substantial empty space either side of the prose. **This is correct.** A reading column that expands with the viewport becomes unreadable, and the emptiness reads as composure rather than waste — provided the type is well set.

## Vertical rhythm — the primary structural tool

Almost everything is a vertical sequence, so vertical space carries most of the structure.

```
between paragraphs         base unit
between movements          noticeably larger than base — this is the section marker
before uncertainty         identical to other movement gaps (position, not emphasis)
after title                small — masthead belongs to the heading
after masthead             larger — the argument begins
around inline insets       more above than below (attaches to preceding text)
between index items        tight and regular — a scanning cadence
around artifacts           larger — a change of register after prose ends
```

**The critical relationship: inter-movement space must be unmistakably larger than inter-paragraph space.** This is how the four movements of an investigation become perceptible without a single heading (VP-5). If the ratio is too small, the piece reads as undifferentiated text; too large, and it fragments into sections.

**Uncertainty gets no extra space.** Its position is the marker, per VP-4. Adding space would be emphasis by another route.

## Space by surface

| Surface | Density | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Investigation detail** | Open | Sustained reading |
| **Investigation index** | **Tight** | Scanned, not read. A spacious list of eight items looks like an attempt to fill a page |
| **Notes index** | Tight–moderate | Short content close together; spread out, it looks lost |
| **Homepage** | Open at top, tightening downward | Orientation needs air; the index below does not |
| **About** | Moderate | Reference material, read once |
| **Masthead apparatus** | **Dense** | Density is the contrast with prose, and the system's visual signature |

**Density is not the enemy of quality.** The index *should* be dense. Editorial whitespace belongs around prose, not around metadata — see `DENSITY_AND_WHITESPACE.md`.

---

## Content-length adaptation

Per VP-6: **layout adapts to content; content is never padded.**

- No minimum heights anywhere.
- A 150-word investigation occupies 150 words of vertical space.
- The masthead may be visually comparable in mass to a very short investigation's prose. **That is honest** — a short piece with full context is a real thing.
- The homepage at one investigation is short. It should be.

---

## What space must not do

| Prohibited | Why |
|---|---|
| Create luxury | Whitespace here serves pacing and hierarchy, not prestige |
| Substitute for content | Space around thin content advertises the thinness |
| Vary between investigations | Consistency makes position learnable; variation is novelty at the cost of navigation |
| Centre prose text | Centred body text harms reading |
| Full-viewport hero areas | Delays content for a reader who came to read |

---

## The two tests

**1.** Remove all colour, rules and boxes. **Is the structure still clear?** If not, space is not doing its job.

**2.** Squint at the page. **Is the largest area of visual mass reading matter?** If chrome or metadata dominates, the allocation has inverted.
