# Three Complete Creative Directions

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 4) · The three finalists from `CREATIVE_TERRITORIES.md`, developed fully.

---

# CD-1 — THE WORKING DOCUMENT

**Core idea:** every investigation is a working document with a visible apparatus. The main column carries the reasoning; a secondary register carries time, status, uncertainty, asides and later revisions.

**Experience principle:** *the thinking about the work is visible alongside the work.*

**Brand fit:** the highest possible. "Reasoning, shown" stops being a slogan and becomes layout.

**Visual language:** two registers held in a constant relationship. Near-monochrome warm neutral. No rules, no boxes — the registers are distinguished by type and position alone.
**Typographic direction:** editorial serif for prose · technical sans for apparatus · mono for literal values.
**Colour philosophy:** warm editorial neutral; one accent at ~2%, reserved for revision markers and current status.
**Spatial language:** functional asymmetry. Constant reading column, constant apparatus position. Vertical rhythm marks the four movements without headings.
**Interaction language:** documentary. Links, scrolling, filtering at volume.
**Motion language:** near-none. State changes only.

**Investigation experience:** Approach B — document with apparatus. Uncertainty marked by consistent position rather than by any visual device.
**Notes experience:** the same form compressed — often apparatus plus two sentences. Very short notes render inline in the index.
**Artifact experience:** an apparatus entry appearing after the reasoning concludes. Absent artifacts render nothing.
**Homepage experience:** H1 — a plain identity line, then the most recent investigation given real space, then an index preview showing apparatus.
**Navigation:** four persistent links; the site name is the home link.
**Mobile:** apparatus folds inline at the point in the prose it relates to. Nothing behind a drawer.
**Accessibility:** strong — everything is text in a defined reading order; nothing depends on position or colour.
**Performance:** minimal. Text, type, no images at launch, functionally zero JS.

**Return-visit mechanism:** **the apparatus records revisions with dates.** A returning reader sees not just new work but *changed thinking* — which no other direction provides.

**Genericity risk:** low. **Corporate risk:** low. **Gimmick risk:** low–moderate — the apparatus becoming a styled but empty gutter.
**Scalability:** excellent. Identical at 1 and at 50; the apparatus scales down to bare metadata.

**Why it feels like Het:** it is the only direction whose central device *requires* candid content. An apparatus with no revisions, no stated uncertainty and no asides is visibly empty. The design cannot be worn by someone who does not work this way.

# CD-2 — EDITORIAL INVESTIGATION

**Core idea:** each investigation is a piece of technical journalism — a question, a turn, an honest ending — set to magazine standards.

**Experience principle:** *the work deserves to be read properly.*

**Brand fit:** high. Matches the reasoned first-person voice directly.

**Visual language:** classical long-form editorial. Strong opening typography, generous measure, clear article architecture.
**Typographic direction:** editorial serif dominant; large expressive display; sans for metadata only; mono for code.
**Colour philosophy:** warm editorial neutral, or near-monochrome. Single scarce accent.
**Spatial language:** classical editorial rhythm, generous margins, single column.
**Interaction language:** minimal — reading and following.
**Motion language:** none beyond page transitions.

**Investigation experience:** Approach A — editorial narrative. Continuous prose; findings as a turn; limitations as the closing movement.
**Notes experience:** short column pieces.
**Artifact experience:** a plainly-set block near the end.
**Homepage experience:** H1, more editorially framed — the featured investigation opens like a feature.
**Navigation:** four links, quiet.
**Mobile:** excellent — long-form reading is phone-native.
**Accessibility:** excellent. **Performance:** excellent.

**Return-visit mechanism:** new pieces. Weaker — no mechanism for showing *changed* thinking.

**Genericity risk:** **moderate.** "Editorial portfolio" is a recognised and increasingly common look.
**Corporate risk:** low. **Gimmick risk:** very low.
**Scalability:** good, though the index becomes a plain list with no additional dimension.

**Why it feels like Het:** the voice carries it — but only the voice. The visual system would work unchanged for any thoughtful writer in any field.

# CD-3 — THE FIELD NOTEBOOK

**Core idea:** a practitioner's notebook — dated, sequential, partly unfinished, occasionally corrected.

**Experience principle:** *this is a record kept while working, not a portfolio assembled afterwards.*

**Brand fit:** high on personality, moderate on credibility.

**Visual language:** entry-based, date-led. A strong left date rail. Corrections visible as corrections.
**Typographic direction:** humanist sans or serif; the date system is itself a design element.
**Colour philosophy:** warm neutral; accent reserved for revisions.
**Spatial language:** vertical sequence, compact entries, tight rhythm.
**Interaction language:** browsing and scanning.
**Motion language:** minimal.

**Investigation experience:** a longer entry among shorter ones — the distinction is length, not form.
**Notes experience:** **the native form.** This direction is built around them.
**Artifact experience:** attached to an entry.
**Homepage experience:** closest to H4 — the recent record is the homepage.
**Navigation:** possibly three items; the record is the site.
**Mobile:** excellent. **Accessibility:** strong. **Performance:** excellent.

**Return-visit mechanism:** strong — the record visibly extends.

**Genericity risk:** low. **Corporate risk:** very low.
**Gimmick risk:** **moderate–high.** Skeuomorphic paper, torn edges or handwriting would be fatal, and the metaphor pulls that way.
**Scalability:** good numerically, **poor qualitatively** — the strongest investigation sinks beneath recent notes within months. This is the weakness Kettle's thematic clustering exists to solve.

**Why it feels like Het:** maximum personality, and incompleteness is native rather than awkward. But it undersells verified professional work — a notebook reads as informal to the GRC audience, which is a primary audience.

# Radical differentiation test (Part 23)

> *If the exact same visual system were applied to another cybersecurity student, would it still work?*

**CD-1 — No, and this is the decisive result.** The apparatus requires dated revisions, stated uncertainty, and honest status. Applied to a portfolio of finished, confident project descriptions, it would render a visibly empty gutter — the design would announce the absence. **The visual system depends on the content behaviour.**

**CD-2 — Yes.** Editorial typography is content-agnostic. A well-set long-form site works for any thoughtful writer. Differentiation lives entirely in the voice.

**CD-3 — Mostly yes.** A dated notebook works for anyone who keeps one. Differentiation lives in content honesty, not in the system.

> *What part of the experience is impossible to separate from Het's actual content and reasoning?*

**CD-1: the apparatus.** It holds four things only Het can supply — when he did the work, how settled he considers it, what he could not determine, and what he later changed his mind about. Three of those four are unusual enough that most portfolios have nothing to put there.

**In CD-1, content + structure + voice + visual language reinforce one another.** In CD-2 and CD-3 the visual language is separable from the rest. That is the requirement Part 23 asks for, and only one direction meets it.
