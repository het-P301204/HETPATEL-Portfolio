# Colour Philosophies

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 4) · **No colours selected.** Philosophies only.

**The measured anchor:** on the award-winning site in the research, the accent appears on **8 of ~570 elements — roughly 2%.** Charcoal on 376, white on 157. That ratio, not a hue, is the inheritance. An accent used everywhere is a theme; used rarely, it is a signal.

---

## C1 — Restrained monochrome

**Idea:** a single ink on a single ground. Emphasis by weight, size and space alone.
**Emotional effect:** austere, serious, confident.
**Credibility:** very high.
**Accessibility:** trivially strong — contrast is the only variable.
**Cybersecurity association:** none, which is an advantage.
**Genericity risk:** low–moderate. Pure monochrome is itself a recognisable editorial choice.
**Light mode:** natural. **Dark mode:** natural.
**Accent:** none — status and domain rely entirely on typography.
**Weakness:** with no accent at all, the apparatus has one fewer tool to distinguish itself, and `unresolved` status has no quiet way to register.

---

## C2 — Warm editorial neutral ⭐

**Idea:** a warm paper-toned ground with a near-black ink, and one restrained accent used at roughly the measured 2%.
**Emotional effect:** calm, considered, human. Warmth without informality.
**Credibility:** high — the register of a well-made book or journal.
**Accessibility:** strong; warm neutrals hold contrast well.
**Cybersecurity association:** **none — deliberately.** This is the furthest possible position from the category default while remaining serious.
**Genericity risk:** low. Warm neutrals are essentially absent from the security category.
**Light mode:** primary and natural. **Dark mode:** a warm dark ground, never pure black.
**Accent:** one, scarce. Used for links, current status, and revision markers.
**Weakness:** warmth could read as insufficiently technical to some readers. Mitigated by mono for literal values and by precise language.

---

## C3 — Technical dark/light duality

**Idea:** both modes designed as first-class, with dark as the default.
**Emotional effect:** contemporary, technical, familiar to practitioners.
**Credibility:** high with technical audiences.
**Accessibility:** achievable but doubles the contrast work.
**Cybersecurity association:** **strong — which is the problem.** Dark-by-default is the single clearest signal of category membership. Measured: `rgb(18,19,26)` on zyekh, black on adharvkt.
**Genericity risk:** **high.** This is where the category already sits.
**Accent:** typically one cool hue — and cool accents on dark grounds are precisely the saturated combination.
**Weakness:** it concedes the most distinctive available position for familiarity.

---

## C4 — Functional colour encoding

**Idea:** colour carries meaning — status, domain, or origin each with a hue.
**Emotional effect:** systematic, interface-like.
**Credibility:** moderate.
**Accessibility:** **poor by default.** Colour-encoded meaning fails Principle 10 (must survive being read aloud) unless fully duplicated in text — at which point the colour is redundant.
**Genericity risk:** moderate. **Corporate risk:** high — this is the badge system.
**Verdict:** ❌ **rejected.** It contradicts an established principle and reproduces the corporate-sameness pattern measured in Phase 1.

## Recommended: C2 — Warm editorial neutral, with a scarce accent

**Why:**

1. **It is the furthest position from the category default that remains entirely serious.** Every measured cybersecurity site sits on black or near-black. A warm neutral ground is not a stylistic preference here — it is the clearest available differentiation, and it costs nothing in credibility.
2. **It serves long-form reading**, which is the primary activity.
3. **It supports the apparatus** — one scarce accent gives the working register a quiet way to mark revisions and current status without badges.
4. **It is unambiguously not "cyber"**, which serves the GRC audience directly.

**Rules:**
- **Accent at ~2% of elements.** Measured target, not a feeling.
- **One accent.** A second is a theme.
- **Colour never carries meaning alone** — Principle 10.
- **Dark mode is warm dark, never pure black, never neon-accented.**
- Both modes supported; **the identity must survive the switch.** If Het looks like a different person in dark mode, the identity is in the wrong layer.

**Deferred to Phase 5:** specific values, contrast ratios, accent hue, and default mode.

**One honest caution:** warm neutrals are strongly associated with editorial and design portfolios. The differentiation gained against the *security* category is partly spent against the *portfolio* category. It remains the right trade — Het's audience is security, not design — but the palette alone will not carry distinctiveness. **Typography and the apparatus have to do that work.**
