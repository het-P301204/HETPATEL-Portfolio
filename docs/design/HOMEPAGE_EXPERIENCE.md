# Homepage Experience

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 4) · Five opening experiences. Information sequence is fixed by Phase 3; these differ in *how the visitor encounters it*.

---

## H1 — THE OPEN INVESTIGATION

**First impression:** the most recent investigation, given real space — its question, a portion of the reasoning, and one thing Het couldn't determine. Het's name and discipline sit above it, quiet.
**Identity:** by demonstration. You learn what he does by watching him do it.
**Credibility:** the reasoning itself, plus `origin: professional` metadata.
**Curiosity:** the unresolved element is the hook — an open question is more interesting than a finished claim.
**Depth:** the investigation continues; the index follows.
**Personality:** immediate — the first substantial thing on the page is his voice.
**Return:** the featured investigation changes as work accrues.

**Strength:** the fastest possible route to the brand promise. A visitor who reads only the homepage has still *seen* the behaviour.
**Weakness:** a recruiter meets content before orientation. Mitigated if the identity line above is genuinely clear.
**At 1 investigation:** works perfectly. **At 50:** works perfectly.

---

## H2 — THE STANDING QUESTION

**First impression:** a single question Het is currently working on, set large, with a date. Below it, quietly: what he does.
**Identity:** inferred from the kind of question he asks.
**Credibility:** deferred to the investigations below.
**Curiosity:** very high — an open question invites.
**Depth:** the question links to its investigation.
**Personality:** high — the choice of question *is* the personality.
**Return:** **the strongest mechanism of the five** — the standing question changes and the change is the reason to come back.

**Strength:** genuinely distinctive; nothing in the corpus opens this way. Directly expresses the investigator archetype.
**Weakness:** **requires Het to always have a live open question.** If it goes stale for six months, the homepage broadcasts stagnation more loudly than any other option. It also gives recruiters nothing in the first five seconds.
**Risk:** it is a cadence promise in disguise — exactly what `EMPTY_STATE_AND_GROWTH.md` warns against.

## H3 — THE PLAIN STATEMENT

**First impression:** name, and two or three sentences of what he does and where he has done it. Nothing else above the fold.
**Identity:** immediate and explicit.
**Credibility:** stated, then evidenced below.
**Curiosity:** low — nothing pulls.
**Depth:** a list of investigations follows.
**Personality:** in the writing only.
**Return:** the list changes.

**Strength:** perfect for the recruiter journey; maximum clarity; works at any volume.
**Weakness:** **the least memorable.** A visitor could leave without encountering any reasoning — failing the 5-minute memory test. This is the conventional solution and it earns nothing.

---

## H4 — THE WORKING INDEX

**First impression:** the full index of investigations, with apparatus visible — dates, status, domain, origin — as the first thing on the page. Identity above it in one line.
**Identity:** brief; the work does the talking.
**Credibility:** range and status honesty are visible at a glance.
**Curiosity:** moderate — titles must carry it.
**Depth:** immediate; everything is one click away.
**Personality:** in the titles and in the presence of `unresolved` entries.
**Return:** new rows, changed statuses — visible instantly.

**Strength:** most efficient for practitioners; return-visit legibility is excellent.
**Weakness:** **fails badly at low volume.** An index of three rows as the homepage looks like an unfinished site. It is also the Phase 1 "Index" failure — austerity reads as confident from a known name, unfinished from an unknown one.
**At 1–5 investigations:** poor. **At 25+:** strong.

---

## H5 — THE TWO REGISTERS

**First impression:** a split opening — what he does professionally on one side, what he is investigating independently on the other. Both dated.
**Identity:** the dual position (testing / regulated assurance) is the first thing understood.
**Credibility:** professional work is immediately visible.
**Curiosity:** moderate.
**Depth:** two entry paths.
**Personality:** moderate.
**Return:** either register may change.

**Strength:** communicates the positioning faster than any other option.
**Weakness:** **the independent register is currently near-empty.** A two-column opening with one column thin advertises the gap — a direct violation of "sections disappear rather than emptying." It also hard-codes a positioning Het may move away from.

## Recommended: H1 — The Open Investigation

**Why:** it is the only opening that *demonstrates* the brand rather than describing it, while working identically at one investigation and at fifty. A visitor who reads nothing else has still seen reasoning and an admitted limit — which is precisely the 5-minute memory the site is designed to produce.

**Borrowing from H3:** the identity line above the investigation must be genuinely plain and complete — name, discipline, that the work is professional. This is what rescues the recruiter journey, and it is why H1 scores 7 rather than 4 there.

**Borrowing from H4:** below the featured investigation, the index preview shows apparatus — dates, status, domain — so range and honesty are visible without opening anything.

**Rejected from H2:** the standing question. Genuinely the most distinctive idea in this set, and it is a cadence promise. Het's writing capacity is unverified, and a homepage that visibly stales is worse than one that does not move. **The idea is retained in a weaker, safer form:** an `open` investigation is featured in preference to a closed one when both are recent.

**At one investigation:** H1 is the whole homepage — identity line, the investigation, contact. No index preview, no notes section. Complete, and honestly small.
