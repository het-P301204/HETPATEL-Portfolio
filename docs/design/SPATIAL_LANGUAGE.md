# Spatial Language

**Date:** 2026-08-14 (Phase 4)

---

## The governing decision: functional asymmetry

The site has **two registers of content** — reasoning and apparatus. They are not equal, and they should not be spatially equal.

**Asymmetry here is not a style choice.** It follows from the content model: a main column carrying prose, and a secondary register carrying time, status, uncertainty and revision. Arbitrary asymmetry is banned; asymmetry that encodes a real distinction is the design.

This is the difference between "asymmetric because it looks contemporary" and "asymmetric because these two things are different kinds of thing."

---

## Space by content type

| Content | Spatial treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **Investigation prose** | Single column at a comfortable reading measure. Generous vertical rhythm | Long-form reading is the primary activity |
| **Apparatus** | Consistent secondary position; narrower; tighter leading | Must read as a different register without a rule or box |
| **Investigation index** | Dense list. Tight vertical rhythm | Scanning, not reading. Density is appropriate and honest |
| **Field notes** | Compact entries, close together | Short content in a spacious frame looks lost |
| **Homepage** | Generous at the top, tightening downward | Orientation needs air; the index below does not |
| **About** | Linear, moderate | Reference material |

**The most important line here:** density is not the enemy of quality. The index *should* be dense — a spacious list of eight items looks like an attempt to fill a page. Editorial whitespace belongs around prose, not around metadata.

---

## Grid

**A structural grid, not a compositional one.** Its job is to hold the two registers in a consistent relationship, not to enable varied layouts.

- One primary reading column, constant across investigations.
- One apparatus column, constant in position.
- The index uses the full width — a different structure for a different activity.

**Consistency is the point.** A reader who has read one investigation should know where to look in the next. Layout variation between investigations would be novelty at the cost of navigation.

---

## Rhythm

**Vertical rhythm is the primary spatial tool**, since almost everything is a vertical sequence.

- Space between movements of an investigation is larger than space between paragraphs — this is how the four movements are marked *without headings*.
- Space before *what I couldn't determine* is consistent, so its position is learnable.
- The index uses a tighter, regular rhythm — a scanning cadence, not a reading one.

**This is how Principle 3 and Principle 9 are reconciled**: the uncertainty movement is marked by *consistent position and spacing*, never by a box or colour. Space does the work a device would otherwise do.

---

## Length adaptation

**Layout adapts to content length; content is never padded to fill layout** (Principle 4).

- No minimum heights.
- No equal-height rows.
- A 150-word investigation occupies 150 words of space and looks complete.
- The apparatus may be longer than the prose on a short investigation. That is honest and should be allowed to happen.

---

## What is rejected

| Rejected | Why |
|---|---|
| Card grids | Rejected as the primary form; also imposes uniform heights, violating Principle 4 |
| Full-bleed sections | Interrupts reading; belongs to marketing pages |
| Overlapping elements | Decorative asymmetry; harms accessibility and reflow |
| Multi-column prose | Fails on mobile; forces awkward reading on desktop |
| Sticky sidebars | The apparatus belongs beside its paragraph, not pinned |
| Hero sections occupying a full viewport | Delays content for a reader who came to read |
| Parallax / depth layering | Decoration; contradicts the motion principles |

---

## Mobile

**The two-register structure resolves by folding, not collapsing.**

Apparatus entries move inline, positioned at the point in the prose they relate to. Nothing goes behind a drawer or toggle.

**Why this matters:** a sidebar collapsed into a hidden panel would hide the apparatus — and the apparatus carries the uncertainty and revision information that Principle 3 forbids hiding. Folding inline preserves both reading order and meaning.

**Consequence:** the mobile experience is a single, well-set column with occasional inset asides. That is a genuinely good reading experience, not a degraded desktop one.

---

## The test

> **Remove all colour, all rules, all boxes. Is the hierarchy still clear?**

If yes, the spatial system is doing its job and colour is free to be scarce (~2%). If no, the layout is relying on decoration to communicate structure — and Principle 8 says typography and space carry the identity.
